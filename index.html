Why it matters

The digital divide in India isn't just about internet access — it touches education, income, gender, and opportunity. Organisations like Hamari Pahchan are working to close this through programs like GadgetShala.

The Challenge

Why 400 Million Indians Are Still Offline

It's not one problem. It's six problems stacked on each other.

📡
No Signal

25,000+ villages have zero mobile coverage. Getting internet to remote areas costs more than companies can recover.

25K+ villages unconnected
💸
Can't Afford It

A basic smartphone is 2-4 months wages for many rural families. Even cheap data adds up when you earn ₹300 a day.

Avg rural wage: ₹300/day
📖
Don't Know How

First-time users get scammed, confused, or scared off. Without guidance a smartphone becomes useless or even dangerous.

Only 38% digitally literate
👩
Women Left Out

Women are 40% less likely to own a smartphone. Social pressure, safety concerns, and money all play a role.

67% male vs 33% female users

Not in My Language

Most useful content is in English. 90% of India speaks something else first — so the internet feels foreign.

What Can Help

Bridging the Gap

The approaches that actually move the needle — some already happening, some we need more of.

1

Community Gadget Centers — the GadgetShala way

Not everyone can buy a phone. But if there's a shared space in the community with devices, guidance, and a safe environment — that changes everything. That's exactly what GadgetShala does and it works.

GadgetShala Initiative
2

Content in local languages

When the internet speaks your language it becomes useful. Right now most of it doesn't. Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali — reaching people in their own language is one of the fastest ways to bring them in.

Language Access
3

Putting women first

When women get digital access, whole families benefit. Programs designed specifically for women — in safe and respectful spaces — have an outsized ripple effect on education and household income.

Gender Equity
4

Government Wi-Fi rollout (PM-WANI)

India's plan to put public hotspots in post offices, train stations, and village service centers. The infrastructure exists — it just needs to be pushed faster and further.

Policy
5

Refurbished phones and laptops

Collect donated devices, fix them, give them to students or low-income families. Tackles cost problem. Reduces e-waste. Works right now without waiting for prices to drop.

Circular Economy
6

Teaching people to stay safe online

Scams and fake news push new users away from the internet entirely. Cyber safety education builds the trust needed to actually participate — not just connect and disconnect.

Digital Safety

My Internship Inspiration

I joined Hamari Pahchan for my internship and got put on the GadgetShala initiative. Honestly I didn't fully get what it meant at first — but the more I got into it, the more it stuck with me.

GadgetShala is basically a shared device center for communities that can't afford their own gadgets. Sounds simple. But for someone using a smartphone for the first time, it genuinely changes what's possible for them.

"When a woman in a rural community sends her first message online, or checks a government scheme she qualifies for — that's not just technology. That's access to a life that was always hers."

I made this dashboard to put real numbers behind what I was seeing on the ground. So people could understand the scale of the problem — not just hear about it.
